A Family-Level Graphical Gatekeeping Procedure with Iterative Retesting

Zhiying Qiu, Wenge Guo, Sanat Sarkar · arxiv

Confirmatory clinical trials often involve multiple objectives grouped into families according to scientific priorities. At the intersection of graphical multiple testing and gatekeeping methods, we develop a Bonferroni-based family-level graphical procedure with partial-rejection-based transfers and iterative re-testing. Families are tested sequentially within each stage, using fixed component tests and a prespecified transition matrix. The updating rule combines current-stage forward contributions based on current family levels with previous-stage backward contributions based on initial allocations. We establish strong global familywise error rate control at a prespecified level under arbitrary dependence. The construction permits backward feedback while retaining fixed component tests and transition coefficients, distinguishing its implementation from forward-only family-level procedures and the specified superchain algorithm. Simulations demonstrate gains over the corresponding No-retest procedure, while identifying an efficiency cost relative to a hypothesis-level graphical comparator with matched initial allocations and edge weights. Clinical illustrations show how the procedure implements family-level priorities and feedback, and an extension accommodates a two-layer structure with multiple families per layer.

Inverse Confounding Analysis: Part 2. Applications to Real- World Observational Data

Sergey Porotsky · arxiv

Unmeasured confounding factors in observational data may lead to biased estimates of the effect of an exposure on an outcome. Consequently, a central problem in causal inference based on observational data is sensitivity analysis with respect to unmeasured confounding. Existing sensitivity-analysis methods generally focus on worst-case bounds. We propose an exact method for quantifying the significance of confounding, defined here in terms of the complete range of analytical values of the stratification-based Risk Ratio over the set of all joint distributions compatible with the observed characteristics. We refer to the proposed method as Inverse Confounding Analysis (ICA) - it is described in [1]. This paper presents examples of applying ICA to several real-world statistical datasets and examines an important issue that has attracted criticism of the method: the practical feasibility of obtaining the input data required for its application. The results are compared with those of two other methods for confounding analysis, the E-value and the method proposed by Pena.

A Geometric Perspective for Marginal R2: Estimation and Inference in Generalized Linear Mixed Models

Hao Qin, Ayumi Mizuno, Kyle Morrison, and 2 others · arxiv

Generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs) are widely used across scientific disciplines, yet quantifying response variation explained by auxiliary information remains challenging for non-Gaussian outcomes and hierarchical dependence. Existing $R^2$ measures provide useful summaries, but their variance-based constructions are not generally linked to a common geometric interpretation, a fixed-design marginal estimand, and corresponding statistical inference. We propose marginal quasi-$R^2$ through two distinct operations. First, marginalization integrates over latent random effects, retaining their uncertainty in the marginal response moments and specifying the model for explanatory assessment. Second, a Quasi-GLM formulation supplies a local working information metric under which orthogonal projection yields an Pythagorean decomposition of explained and unexplained variation. We define a fixed-design population estimand, establish consistency and asymptotic normality of its estimator, and derive cluster-robust sandwich standard errors and confidence intervals under stated regularity conditions. Simulations assess estimation accuracy and interval coverage across response families and random-effects structures. Two ecological applications illustrate the importance of reporting estimation uncertainty alongside marginal quasi-$R^2$. The method is implemented in the R package quasiR2.

FADO: Learning Causal Explanations with Prior-Fitted Networks

Seth Flaxman, Jess Carr, Annabel Jakob, and 2 others · arxiv

In regression and classification tasks with tabular datasets, most of the literature on explanation in ML (e.g. SHAP, permutation importance) has focused on predictive attribution: does feature $X_i$ help predict outcome $Y$? At its heart, predictive attribution captures correlation, not causation. We instead ask a causal question: would manipulating feature $X_i$ change outcome $Y$? We define a natural quantity to answer this question, the feature intervention effect: $\Delta_i = E[Y\mid do(X_i=+1)]-E[Y\mid do(X_i=-1)]$. In general, estimating this quantity from observational data alone would entail strong causal assumptions. We propose FADO (Feature-wise Amortized Do-Operators), an amortized inference method using prior-fitted networks trained on synthetic tabular datasets generated by structural causal models. During training, the synthetic data-generating process is known, so we can compute ground-truth intervention effects $\Delta_i$, and train a model to predict them at test time from a purely observational tabular dataset. As we demonstrate through extensive experiments, our new causal explainability method is easy to train and provides better explanations under a range of settings for which standard predictive attribution fails, including proxy variables, confounders, colliders, and placebos. We evaluate it on real, synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, with and without hidden confounders, and compare it to existing predictive attribution methods and state-of-the-art causal inference methods.

Improving Variance Estimation for Covariate Adjustment with Binary Outcomes

Kaitlyn Lee, Alex Ocampo, Courtney Schiffman, and 3 others · arxiv

Covariate adjustment is a general method for improving precision when estimating treatment effects in randomized trials and is recommended by the FDA in its 2023 guidance when baseline variables are prognostic for the primary outcome. We focus on a method highlighted in that guidance called ``standardization" (or ``g-computation") for estimating the marginal treatment effect. We address the question of how to reliably estimate variance for binary outcomes when marginal outcome probabilities are close to 0 or 1. We propose an influence function-based leave-one-out cross-validated (IF-LOO) variance estimator for the standardized difference-in-means average treatment effect. Through simulation studies, we show that this estimator provides appropriate type-I error control and performs reliably in challenging settings where existing methods can yield inflated type-I error or fail entirely, such as when outcome events are rare or sample sizes are small. In addition to having desirable statistical properties, we derive a closed-form expression for the proposed estimator, enabling straightforward and reliable implementation by study statisticians. The robust finite-sample performance and ease of implementation suggest the IF-LOO variance estimator is a prudent default choice for standardization in clinical trials.

Improving control over unobservables with network data

Vincent Starck · arxiv

This paper develops a method to conduct causal inference in the presence of unobserved confounders by leveraging networks with homophily, a frequently observed tendency to form edges with similar nodes. I introduce a concept of asymptotic homophily, according to which individuals' selectivity scales with the size of the potential connection pool. The resulting network formation model accommodates common empirical features such as homophily, degree heterogeneity, sparsity, and clustering, and provides a framework to obtain consistent estimators of treatment effects that are robust to selection on unobservables. In an application, I recover an estimate of the effect of parental involvement on students' test scores that is greater than that of OLS, arguably due to the estimator's ability to account for unobserved ability.

No prior over nuisance parameters is required for credible intervals and Bayes factors

Tim Ritmeester · arxiv

This work shows that there are procedures that maintain the subjective Bayesian property of coherently informing decision making under uncertainty (i.e., if they tell you to take a certain bet, you should) without needing to assign a prior distribution over nuisance parameters, only over the parameter(s) of interest. We first carefully define this sense of 'credibility', and show that within this framework one can condition on any statistic of choice rather than only on the full dataset. This relaxation of credibility is a natural middle ground between Bayesian and frequentist statistics in the following sense: To a p% Bayesian credible set we should assign a p% belief after observing both the dataset and the interval, to p% frequentist sets we can generally only assign a p% belief before observing either the data or the interval, while to the statistical sets proposed here we can assign a p% belief after observing the set, but not necessarily after inspecting the full dataset ourselves. We then show that choosing a conditioning statistic whose distribution we know, or can bound, for each value of the parameter of interest leads to prior-free Bayes factors even for composite hypotheses, and to non-parametric credible sets with priors only over the parameter of interest. We derive such procedures for several (groups of) statistical problems. Particularly noteworthy is that any frequentist test gives a Bayes factor of the power over the size of the test, and that anytime-validity of E-process-based tests can be carried over to this Bayes factor.

On Ignorability Beyond Missing at Random: Completion-Invariant Missingness for Nonmonotone Missing Data

Kosuke Morikawa, Yutaka Kano, Shonosuke Sugasawa · arxiv

The missing-at-random assumption underlies most analyses of incomplete data. Under nonmonotone missingness, it is especially stringent, imposing pattern-specific restrictions with different conditioning sets. A strictly weaker normalization condition is known to justify maximizing the observed-data likelihood. We show that every law satisfying this condition has a unique missing-at-random counterpart sharing its observed-data and complete-data distributions. Observed-data procedures developed under missing at random for identified functionals, including doubly robust estimators, therefore remain valid, with the same semiparametric efficiency bound. We then prove that the condition holds if and only if filling in the missing values from their conditional distribution under the true full model preserves the complete-data distribution, hence the name completion-invariant missingness. We use a characteristic kernel to compare the completed distribution with the fitted model and assess the joint compatibility of the specified full model and completion-invariant missingness. We derive the asymptotic null distribution with estimated parameters and establish bootstrap and multiplier calibration. Since a parametric full model alone need not identify completion-invariant missingness, we give identification conditions for Gaussian, Student $t$ and finite mixture families. Simulations assess sensitivity to departures from each assumption, and applications to clinical laboratory measurements and childhood growth illustrate the diagnostic.

MediPFN: Amortized In-Context Nuisance Learning and Inference for Causal Mediation Analysis

Shi Bo, Jiaming Hu · arxiv

Causal mediation analysis decomposes a treatment effect into indirect pathways through mediators and direct pathways through other processes. Tabular foundation models perform classification and regression through in-context learning, providing estimates of conditional means and propensity scores used in causal mediation analysis. Constructing confidence intervals from these estimates requires accounting for uncertainty introduced by fitting and combining several nuisance functions. We introduce MediPFN, an amortized inference framework that adapts a pretrained tabular foundation model to nuisance prediction and combines its predictions through a cross-fitted influence-function estimator. A separate head learns quantiles of the estimator's standardized error from simulated tables with known indirect effects. Supervised fine-tuning, symmetrization, and independent calibration then produce a confidence interval. We establish coverage bounds for the resulting intervals under standard assumptions. Across experiments, MediPFN attains coverage closer to the nominal level than several comparators. It also reduces the computational cost of uncertainty quantification, running 59.1 times faster than bootstrap in matched comparisons.

Inference After Model Refutation: Uniform Post-Relaxation Inference for Partially Identified Models

Ulrich Hounyo · arxiv

When data reject an econometric model, the researcher must decide which identifying assumptions to give up. If several equally preferred repairs restore compatibility, the repair itself is unknown, and inference under any one of them can exclude structural values supported by the others, even when that inference is exact. I propose Post-Relaxation Projection (PR), which carries one simultaneous confidence event through every repair that may be minimal. With repair-by-repair primitive bounds, PR equals the union of all post-relaxation targets compatible with the band, so no smaller band-based set has the same guarantee. I derive limits on learning the repair, the exact perturbation radius of the minimal-repair correspondence, oracle-equivalent adaptation, and a reduced-form version valid at zero first stages. In cross-country data on institutions and income, the data do not settle which historical instrument should be dropped, yet every admissible minimum repair implies a positive effect of institutions.

Hypothesis testing for estimable parameters with missing data: available cases or complete cases?

M. Romero-Madro\~nal, M. D. Jim\'enez-Gamero, B. Milo\v{s}evi\'c · arxiv

Whether to base inferences on available-case (AC) or complete-case (CC) estimators is a long-standing practical dilemma when data are missing. This issue has been investigated for point estimation in specific problems, but hypothesis testing has received comparatively little attention. The tools needed to address this problem are developed in a fully nonparametric framework for hypotheses concerning parameters that can be estimated through functions of vectors of $U$-statistics, under a MCAR missingness mechanism. The joint asymptotic normality of the AC estimators is established, permitting availability probabilities to vary with the sample size. The limiting covariance matrix factorizes into the complete-data covariance matrix and a matrix determined solely by the availability pattern, with both components admitting consistent estimators. This fact enables the construction of a Wald-type test statistic. The CC approach arises as a special case. These results allow AC and CC based tests to be compared, although the comparison is problem specific, which limits its practical value. Rather than selecting one approach, a test based on the maximum of the two statistics is also considered, whose critical value can consistently approximated by two bootstrap procedures. In simulations, its power lies between those of the AC and CC approaches.

Finite Sample Inference in Incomplete Models

Lixiong Li, Marc Henry · arxiv

We propose confidence regions for the parameters of incomplete models with exact coverage of the true parameter in finite samples. Our confidence region inverts a test, which generalizes Monte Carlo tests to incomplete models. The test statistic is a discrete analogue of a new optimal-transport formulation of the structural model. Both test statistic and critical values rely on simulation draws from the distribution of latent variables and are computed using solutions to discrete optimal transport, hence linear programming problems. We also propose a fast preliminary search in the parameter space with an alternative, more conservative yet consistent test, based on a parameter-free critical value. We show that this test is maximally consistent in the following sense: under infinite exchangeability, it consistently rejects every data-generating process that some test with finite-sample size control consistently rejects. We compare size and power of our procedure with competing proposals in simulations based on a regression with interval valued regressors. A second simulation based on latent consideration sets shows that dependent selection can severely distort inference calibrated under i.i.d. sampling, while our procedure retains finite-sample size control. Finally, we apply our methodology to the model of airline entry and price competition in Ciliberto et al. [2021].

Methods: We conducted a focused methodological synthesis updated through May 31, 2026. Because contemporaneous records of the initial exploratory search were unavailable, we conducted a structured verification search using domain-restricted queries targeting PubMed/PubMed Central records and publisher pages. Terms covered implementation strategies, cluster-randomized and stepped-wedge trials, factorial/MOST designs, SMARTs, and adaptive implementation. The accompanying tools provide practical guidance for investigators, implementation scientists, trialists, biostatisticians, funders, and research design cores, including CTSA-supported design and biostatistics cores. Debiased Counterfactual Generation via Flow Matching from Observations

Hugh Dance, Johnny Xi, Peter Orbanz, Benjamin Bloem-Reddy · arxiv

Estimating the full counterfactual distribution under an intervention is central to treatment risk assessment and counterfactual generation tasks. Existing approaches model the counterfactual distribution as a standalone generative target, without exploiting its relationship to the observational data. In this work, we show that under standard assumptions, observational distributions and marginal counterfactual distributions over outcomes are tightly linked: they have identical support and tail behavior, remain statistically close under weak confounding, and share any outcome features which are invariant to confounders. These properties motivate learning counterfactual distributions not from scratch, but via a deconfounding flow from the observational distribution. We formulate this problem via flow-matching and derive a semiparametrically efficient estimator based on a novel efficient influence function correction. We subsequently extend our estimator to target minimal-energy flows in high-dimensions, which we show can be especially simple targets between observational and counterfactual distributions. In experiments, deconfounding flows outperform existing debiased counterfactual distribution estimators, while also mitigating known failure modes of flow-based methods.

Lost in the Bins: Treatment Effects with Discretized Covariates

Cenchen Liu · arxiv

Mean independence restrictions are widely used to identify average treatment effects, but the covariates used to formulate these restrictions are often observed or used in coarsened form. This paper shows that mean independence conditional on a finely measured covariate need not hold after the covariate is replaced by a coarsened version. Coarsening can therefore change the identifying content of the comparison rather than only the precision of estimation. I characterize the resulting coarsening bias for the ATT and ATE. For the ATT, the bias is driven by within-cell composition in untreated potential outcomes. For the ATE, an additional treatment effect heterogeneity term appears. I then introduce $c$-mean dependence, a nonparametric relaxation of exact mean independence at the coarsened level, and derive sharp bounds for average treatment effects. The parameter $c$ determines how far comparisons may extend across neighboring values of the coarsened covariate. I propose sample analogue estimators and bootstrap inference procedures for the resulting bound functions. In an application to voluntary military service using data from Angrist (1998), positive effects on early earnings, which primarily reflect military pay, hold even under the weakest form of $c$-mean dependence, whereas the evidence on later effects on civilian earnings is inconclusive once mean independence is relaxed.

Complete-Association Factorizations of Discrete Copulas: Existence, Tree Characterization, and Log-Linear Representations

Pablo A. Mitnik, Junchao Tang · socarxiv

For multivariate categorical variables, the discrete-copula and log-linear approaches provide two different representations of dependence: the former uses probability distributions with discrete-uniform one-way margins, and the latter uses interaction parameters computed from full-table or marginal distributions. This article studies the exact division of the dependence represented by a positive discrete copula into factors that are themselves complete associations among disjoint groups of variables treated as composites. Although no nesting, tree, ordering, or binary structure is imposed in defining admissible factors, every rooted tree on the component variables generates a regular complete-association factorization: the map from the positive discrete-copula space to the product of the individual factor ranges is a smooth bijection with a smooth inverse, and the node factors are globally variation independent. Conversely, every regular complete-association factorization necessarily has this rooted-tree structure. The article also establishes several connections with log-linear analysis. Saturated full-table interactions and discrete copulas encode the same margin-free dependence information, while tree-induced marginal log-linear blocks provide complete, smooth, variation-independent coordinates for individual association factors. Such a factorwise representation cannot be obtained by directly partitioning saturated full-table interactions for a nontrivial tree. The tree factorization guarantees that complete-association factors can be manipulated independently, through recursive application of iterative proportional fitting. A regular parameterization permits direct implementation of such manipulations by substituting parameter blocks if and only if each block is a smooth, invertible transformation of the corresponding tree-induced marginal log-linear block. The article also shows that every multifurcating node admits an exact binary refinement without loss of dependence information, and that refinement, local rotation, and collapse provide smooth transformations between the factor values associated with different rooted trees. The article’s results support structured dependence models, sensitivity analysis, simulation, and counterfactual-based decompositions, including Shapley decompositions.
